The credits-only app, as the acceptance test

The reason for the split: some apps only need credits. The test is that an admin grants credits, they get spent, and spending stops when they run out or at a member's weekly cap, with payment and Stripe absent. LLM calls are one future example of that spending; creating a meteor is the first spender wired.

Two ledgers

The rule: the payment ledger records real money and lives in payment. The credits ledger records credits and lives in credits. When one event touches both, such as buying a bundle, both rows are written in the same transaction as the Stripe event, so one cannot exist without the other.

The new table is payment_ledger, append-only: owner account, kind, signed amount, currency, description, the unique Stripe reference, and the reference of any credits it granted. That last field is how a refund finds the credits to take back.

What this removes: the net-zero card-purchase pair and its lot reason, and the need for a single-currency rule, since the payment ledger stores each row's own currency. Credit refunds still need the purchase to be in the platform currency, as they do today.

Gaps it closes: today the amount paid on an invoice or bundle is never recorded, and refunds of bundles and subscriptions and chargebacks are lost. Those need the invoice id and amount, the charge's customer, and new dispute events from Stripe.

Consequence: the credits ledger is no longer the record of all money; the payment ledger is. A subscription that grants no credits appears only in the payment ledger. A credits-only app simply has no payment ledger.

This is the pattern the repo already uses for ownerNames, startRun and the notifications queue. The web side follows the same rule: services and hooks arrive through mountPaymentWeb. Rendering another slice's components through its web/index.ts stays allowed, as the docs say, so payment's billing page can show credits' sections and the full app looks the same as today.

The seams that need care

1 · The owner record

Every credit table points at payment_account today. Credits gets its own credit_account (organization or user, exactly one). Payment always talks to credits by owner, so no foreign key crosses the slices.

2 · Pricing reads the plan

charge and quote read planKey, which is a Stripe mirror in payment. Credits owns price lists and stores which one applies. Billing sets it through the port, in the same transaction that mirrors the subscription. Without payment, everyone is on the default list.

3 · One transaction across both

A webhook's event claim and its grant must commit together. Credits' write methods keep taking the caller's tx, as grant already does. Locks are always taken payment first, then credits.

4 · Stripe words in the ledger

credit_ledger.stripe_event_id becomes source_event_id. The lot reasons (plan, top_up…) stay in credits as plain vocabulary; card_purchase goes away, and the ledger gains a take_back reason.

5 · Vendor credits straddle the line

This is the original idea and it stays: a seller can refund a buyer in credits usable only at that seller. The lots and their spending are credits. The refund policy is payment's. VendorCreditsService stays in payment and stops reaching lotRepo; that query becomes a credits method on the port.

6 · The summary is two answers

/billing/summary mixes plan and balance, so it splits in two. The marketplace pages get a buyer's vendor holdings through a hook passed to mountPaymentWeb.

7 · Access control

A permission names one entity, so credits needs its own pair on credit_account: one for a person's own credits, one for a company's Manage Billing role. The grant permission stays with the platform owner, so a credits-only app needs a platform organization with an owner.

Credits limited to services

Today a lot goes to the platform (any service) or to one vendor. This adds a third answer: only these services. For example, a grant that pays for meteors and chat but not for search.

PartChange
LotNew nullable credit_lot.service_keys (text[]). Null is everything; {meteor, chat} is those two. Migration 0006_credits has not shipped, so it is regenerated rather than added to.
ChargeDraws only from lots where service_keys is null or service = any(service_keys). The balance a charge checks is what those lots hold, so a refusal for balance can happen while other credits sit unused.
Spend orderWithin the same priority, restricted lots go before general ones; then soonest expiry, then oldest. Otherwise general credits are used up while restricted ones sit idle.
Grantgrant and the admin grant schema take services, checked against the catalog. The Credit grants page gets a “Spend on” multi-select. Plans and bundles stay general for now.
ScreensThe lots table shows what each lot can pay for. The summary and balance card group by list: “€10 for everything · €5 for Create a meteor, Chat”.
UnchangedRefunds and take-backs, which already act on the exact lot. Price lists, which still decide per owner whether a service can run at all: to switch search off completely, leave it out of the owner's price list.
Later featuresA list names what is allowed, so a new service is not paid by old restricted lots. General lots pay for it with nothing to update. “Everything except search” is left out until it is needed.
ProofUnit and PGlite tests: a restricted lot pays for its services and refuses others; restricted lots draw first; a grant naming an unknown service is refused; refund returns to the restricted lot.
Member allocations

An easy way for an organization to control what each member spends on its own platform. Credits are a unit of control here, not money: depending on the organization type they are created by its owner or bought. Either way the member sees the same thing, an amount per period that stops when it is used up.

In the seed, musical projects hold it (create_credits_musical_project, their owner) and companies do not: companies buy, projects create.
CreatingNo job. The first time a member acts or a page reads in a new period, as lazy expiry already works: one lot on the organization's account, reason allocation, keyed by rule, member and period so it is created once. Replace expires it at the period's end; top up never expires it. A member away for three months on top up gets all three on return.
What a member has leftNever stored, read from the ledger. Replace: the amount minus what was spent this period. Top up: the amount for every period since the rule began, minus what was spent since. Changing a top-up amount keeps what the member had left and counts the new amount from the next period.
SpendingThe charge checks the member's rule (amount left, service in the list), refusing as allowance, then draws from the organization's lots as it does today. Soonest expiry first means replace credits go before top-up ones. Members with no rule share the organization's credits as they do today.

Vendor credits stand for a product's price at one credit to one cent, so the product page and the vendor's refund sheet keep showing money.
ScreensThe weekly limits table and sheet become the allocations table and sheet: period, top up or replace, spend on, and “create these credits” for callers holding the permission. A member's own card shows what is left and when it renews.
